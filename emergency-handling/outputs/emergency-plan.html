6. Medical Precautions

Maya — Asthma Monitoring [DEMO-HEALTH-04, GEN-FLOOD]

Watch for: coughing, wheezing, chest tightness, shortness of breath, throat or nose irritation (flooding raises mold and irritant exposure risk). Keep rescue inhaler + spacer immediately accessible at all times. Follow the existing clinician action plan — do not change doses. If mild symptoms occur: calm position, sit upright, use inhaler per plan, monitor for 15 minutes.

Call 911 for Maya if: Breathing worsens despite inhaler use; lips or nails turn blue; confusion; unable to speak full sentences; severe chest pain or distress.

Ruth — Medication & Mobility

Maintain medication routine timing despite power outage. Encourage proactive hydration from clean stored water (elderly have higher dehydration risk and may not feel thirst). Watch for: dizziness, confusion, altered heart rhythm, falls (walker + weakness = elevated fall risk). Cool with damp cloth if no AC and heat is a concern.

Dehydration Signs

  • All ages: Thirst, dry mouth, dark urine, dizziness, headache, reduced urine output.
  • Maya (child): Additionally — irritability, lethargy, fast breathing, sunken eyes.
  • Ruth (elderly): Additionally — confusion, weakness, rapid pulse. Higher risk — prioritise her fluid intake.

Floodwater Contact

Avoid all contact. If skin exposure: rinse with clean stored water. Eye contact: rinse gently and thoroughly. Open wound exposure: irrigate with clean water, cover, and monitor for infection (redness, swelling, warmth, pus, fever). Ingestion: monitor for nausea, vomiting, diarrhea; call 911 if severe.

Minor Wound Care Without Tap Water

Apply firm pressure to stop bleeding. Clean with soap + stored clean water. Cover with clean cloth or first-aid dressing. Watch for infection signs over the following 24–48 hours.

Call 911: Difficulty breathing; severe chest pain; confusion; severe or uncontrolled bleeding; suspected CO poisoning (move to fresh air first); any life-threatening symptom; rising water trapping the household.
Manage at home: Minor cuts with controlled bleeding, mild dust-related cough, mild asthma responding fully to rescue inhaler, mild dehydration when clean fluids are available.

FAQ

Q: Can we use toilet water or household pipes for hand-washing? No. Floodwater may backflow through drains. Use stored clean water + soap, or hand sanitizer.
Q: Can we drink rain barrel water if we use purification tablets? No. Tablets address biological contamination only; chemical and fuel contamination from floodwater cannot be removed by tablets. Use only sealed commercial drinking water.

7. Mental Health & Family Support

Maya (9) — Managing Anxiety

Frame the situation as a family camping challenge with a clear plan. Give her an active, age-appropriate role (water tracker, supply log keeper) to shift from passive fear to helpful participant. Normalize her asthma plan calmly — keep it visible, check in every hour without alarm. Anchor her with grounding facts: "The house is dry, we have water, we are safe together." Scheduled quiet activities: card games, storytelling, torch-light play.

Ruth (72) — Engagement and Dignity

Maintain daily routine including medication timing and listening to radio updates together. Involve her in real decisions (menu selection, supplies inventory) to preserve autonomy. Ensure comfortable, accessible seating and familiar objects nearby. Acknowledge her mobility constraints openly — the household adapts around her, not the other way around.

Alex & Sam — Caregiver Stress

Rotate leadership: one person leads logistics and evacuation decisions, the other supports Maya and Ruth. Explicit handoff every 4 hours prevents decision fatigue and resentment. Accept "good enough" choices under uncertainty — perfect information is not available. Check in with each other briefly: "Are you okay? What do you need?" You are managing well; you are not expected to solve everything.

Family Communication Rhythm

Gather the whole household every 8 hours (morning and evening). Share verified facts only: water status, any radio updates, next concrete step. Ask each person: "What do you need right now?" Keep logistics talk separate from emotional reassurance. No invented timelines for restoration.

Post-Event Follow-Up Signs

Within one month after the event, consider gentle outreach to a family GP or community mental health service if: Maya shows persistent sleep disruption, startle responses to sounds, or avoidance of storms; Ruth withdraws, misses medications, or shows increased fall risk from inattention; Alex or Sam experience intrusive flood-related thoughts when safe, persistent insomnia, or sharp interpersonal conflict around decisions.
